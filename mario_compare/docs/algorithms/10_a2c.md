# 10 · A2C (Advantage Actor-Critic)

**Code:** [`rl/sb3_agents.py`](../../rl/sb3_agents.py) (stable-baselines3), runner [`experiments/run_a2c.py`](../../experiments/run_a2c.py)
**Run:** `python -m experiments.run_a2c --budget 1000000 --seed 0`

## The idea

A2C (the synchronous version of A3C, Mnih et al. 2016) is PPO's simpler
predecessor. Same actor-critic structure — an actor (our 208 → 18 → 12
network) chooses buttons, a critic predicts future reward — but a simpler
update:

- **short** rollouts (16 steps instead of 512),
- **one** gradient step per rollout (PPO takes 4 passes of mini-batches),
- **no clipping**: the step size alone controls how far the policy moves.

Read [09 · PPO](09_ppo.md) first; this page only covers the differences.

## What happens in each update

1. Play 16 steps, sampling buttons from the actor.
2. For each step, the **advantage** = actual discounted reward over those
   steps + the critic's estimate for where the rollout ended − the critic's
   estimate at that step. (`gae_lambda = 1.0`: plain n-step returns.)
3. One gradient step that:
   - raises the log-probability of each press in proportion to its advantage,
   - trains the critic toward the observed returns,
   - adds an entropy bonus (0.01) to keep exploring.
4. Repeat.

At 1M frames: ≈ 240,000 steps ≈ 15,000 small updates.

## A2C vs PPO in one line

A2C makes **many small, cheap, noisy** updates from fresh data and throws it
away; PPO makes **fewer, larger, safer** updates, squeezing each batch of
data for several passes. On a fixed frame budget, PPO usually gets more out of
each frame; A2C is the simpler, more fragile baseline.

## Settings

| flag | default | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `--lr` | 7e-4 | learning rate (SB3 uses RMSprop for A2C) |
| `--n-steps` | 16 | steps per update |
| `--ent-coef` | 0.01 | entropy bonus |

Budget, network export, deterministic testing and episode cap are identical
to PPO.

## What to expect on Mario

- More sensitive to the learning rate than PPO: too high and the policy can
  collapse onto one button (e.g. "always right") and stop exploring.
- 16-step rollouts see only ~64 frames ahead before bootstrapping from the
  critic, so early on (when the critic is poor) learning to jump a pipe that
  pays off many steps later is harder.

## Strengths and weaknesses

- ✅ Simplest actor-critic; fast updates; low memory.
- ❌ No safety clip: unstable with large learning rates.
- ❌ Each frame is used for exactly one gradient step.
